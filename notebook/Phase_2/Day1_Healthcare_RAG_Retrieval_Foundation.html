Day 1 • Enterprise RAG Hands-On

Building a Reliable Healthcare Policy Retrieval Foundation

How do we make sure an AI system retrieves the right policy, for the right plan, from the right section — before an LLM is ever asked to generate an answer?

1. Business Problem Statement

Policy information is fragmented

Healthcare payer rules are distributed across multiple policy documents, benefit plans and operational guides. Similar topics may exist in several documents with different thresholds and conditions.

Same topic, different business rule

A question such as “When does physical therapy require prior authorization?” cannot be answered correctly without knowing the member's plan. Gold PPO and Silver HMO may contain similar wording but different limits.

Keyword search is not enough

Users may ask for “insurer approval” while the policy uses “prior authorization.” Exact-text search can miss semantically equivalent language.

Retrieval errors become generation errors

If the wrong chunk is retrieved, even a strong LLM can produce a confident but business-incorrect response. Retrieval quality therefore becomes a core control point.

2. What We Are Trying to Achieve

Correct Scope

Retrieve policy content for the intended plan, policy domain and version.

Semantic Relevance

Find the right meaning even when user wording differs from document wording.

Traceability

Retain document, section, page and metadata so every result can be explained.

Day 1 principle: RAG quality starts before the LLM call. Good retrieval is a data-engineering and search-design problem first.

3. Participant Activity

Challenge: Make Retrieval Plan-Aware

Business question: “For a Silver HMO member, when does physical therapy require prior authorization?”

  • Run semantic search without a metadata filter.
  • Inspect the top retrieved policy chunks.
  • Apply plan_type = "Silver HMO" as a metadata filter.
  • Rerun the same query and compare results.
  • Change top_k from 1 → 3 → 5 and observe recall versus noise.

5. Expected Business Outcome

Before

Search returns documents that look relevant but may belong to the wrong plan, wrong policy version or wrong operating context.

After

Retrieval is constrained by semantic similarity plus business metadata, increasing the likelihood that downstream answers are based on the right source.
