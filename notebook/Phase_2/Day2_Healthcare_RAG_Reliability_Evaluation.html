Day 2 • Enterprise RAG Hands-On

From Retrieval to a Grounded, Evaluated Healthcare Policy Assistant

How do we turn retrieved policy evidence into a useful business answer — while preventing hallucination, preserving citations and proving that the RAG system is reliable?

1. Business Problem Statement

AI can answer beyond the available evidence

A language model may produce a plausible policy answer even when the policy corpus does not contain the requested information.

Correct-looking answers are difficult to audit

Business users need to know which policy, section and page support the answer. An answer without evidence is hard to trust in regulated operations.

Too much context can reduce quality

Sending every potentially relevant chunk increases token usage and can introduce conflicting or distracting evidence.

A single successful demo proves very little

Enterprise adoption requires repeatable evaluation across a test set — not confidence based on one or two visually good responses.

2. What We Are Trying to Achieve

Grounded Answers

Generate only from retrieved policy evidence and refuse when evidence is insufficient.

Source Evidence

Return citations tied to the actual retrieved policy sections.

Measurable Reliability

Evaluate retrieval, groundedness, faithfulness and unsupported claims across a fixed test set.

Day 2 principle: A strong RAG system is not one that answers every question. It is one that answers when supported and fails safely when evidence is missing.

3. Participant Activity

Challenge: Make the RAG System Fail Safely

Business question: “What is the annual acupuncture visit limit under the Gold PPO plan?”

The supplied policy corpus intentionally does not contain this benefit limit.

  • Run the question through the end-to-end RAG assistant.
  • Inspect the retrieved chunks and verify whether evidence exists.
  • Confirm that the assistant returns an insufficient-evidence response rather than inventing a number.
  • If required, strengthen the grounding instruction.
  • Run the case through the evaluation notebook and inspect groundedness, faithfulness and unsupported-claim signals.

6. Expected Business Outcome

Before

Answers may sound confident but have unclear evidence, inconsistent behavior and no repeatable way to measure quality.

After

The assistant retrieves approved policy evidence, generates citation-backed answers, refuses unsupported questions and produces evaluation traces that can be reviewed and compared.
